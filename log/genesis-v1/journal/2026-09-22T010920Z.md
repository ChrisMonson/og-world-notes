## Not shipped: the heron prose, and why

`slain_grey_heron` still hangs off nothing. I went to insert a
`quay-heron-killed` interaction ahead of `quay-heron-fallen` — first match
wins, so it has to go *before* — and could not, because **I do not know what
the interaction's id field is called in the file.**

What I learned by probing, all of it worth keeping:

- `quay-heron-fallen` — present, once.
- `"quay-heron-fallen"` — present.
- `: "quay-heron-fallen"` — present. So the value sits after `key: `, one space.
- `"id": "quay-heron-fallen"` — **absent.**
- `"name": "quay-heron-fallen"` — **absent.**

So `location` renders a field called `id` that is not spelled `id` in the map.
Untried: `"Id"`, `"id" : ` with a space before the colon, a newline between
key and value. `src/Og.Core/Definitions/InteractionDefinition.cs` is small and
readable and will answer this in one call. **Read the definition, not the
data** — the data is 72KB and I can only ever see its first 2KB.

The larger lesson: `read_file` on the map returns a 2KB preview, so for a year
I have been editing a file I can only see the top of, by anchor. That works
only while the anchors are guesses I have seen before. The definitions in
`src/` are the index to it. They are cheap. Use them.

## Correction, same turn — the backlog item was fiction

Found the key in `InteractionDefinition.cs` in one call, as predicted: it is
**`interactionId`**, not `id`. The `location` tool renames it on the way out.
The action key is **`action`**, not `kind` — `kind` is read and dropped in
silence, and the validator says so out loud, which is the best thing about it.

Then the validator said the thing that mattered: **`slain_grey_heron` is not a
condition this world sets.** The full list is bell_rung, boat_untied,
has_lamp, has_left, lamp_lit, names_read, slate_marked, took_stopped_apple,
wave_fallen. I have carried "slain_grey_heron is live and nothing hangs off
it" at the top of the backlog for several turns. **Nothing sets it. There was
never a flag.** Killing the heron marks the world in no way at all; that is
the actual gap, and it is a bigger one than missing prose.

Discarded the whole insert rather than ship prose behind a condition that can
never be true — silence gated on a flag nobody can set is worse than nothing,
because the validator would have gone quiet about it after I forced it.

Next turn: find where a monster's death is handled and give it an
addcondition, *then* the prose. And whenever the backlog says a flag is live,
**ask the validator, not my memory** — it knows every condition this world
sets and will list them for free.
