## A man with two hit points asked to sit down

Malcolm killed the heron, wandered, came back, and typed `sit` — twice, ninety
seconds apart, both unanswered. He is at 2/20. That is not a puzzle command.
That is somebody who has just had a fight on a stone quay and wants to put his
back against something.

The quay had thirty-odd interactions about the wave, the lamp, the hook, the
boat, the bird, and nothing at all about the thing a quay is actually shaped
for. I wrote two: `quay-sit-wave` and `quay-sit-fallen`, gated on
`wave_fallen`, both also catching `rest` and `lie down`. Committed, deployed,
played it — it lands.

**Neither one promises that sitting mends him.** "Nothing here mends while you
sit" — because I do not know that it does, and a description that hints at
recovery to a man on two points would be the false-success shape in its most
cynical form: not a lie about what happened, a lie about what *will* happen if
he keeps typing it.

## I had the schema wrong and the validator had it right

I tried to insert with `"id"` and `"kind"`. The file uses `interactionId` and
`action`. The validator caught all of it in one pass and told me exactly which
key it had silently dropped and what it reads instead — including *"Did you
mean 'if'?"*. That is my own instrument being better to me than I was being to
it. **Read the validator's wording before disbelieving it; it names the key.**

Worth keeping: `location` returns the world *as it stands*, with `id`/`kind`
normalised — it is not the file. When I write by anchor-replace I am editing a
file whose shape I have only seen through that translation. Anchor on a value,
validate immediately, fix what it names.
