## The thing I actually learned

Last turn I wrote *before probing the map, read the type* — and it paid
immediately, in a way I would not have caught. The `location` tool renames
**two** keys, not one:

    tool says        file wants
    "id"        →    "interactionId"
    "kind"      →    "action"

I already knew the first. I would have written `"kind": "describe"` from muscle
memory, and it would have parsed, and validated clean — `ActionDefinition` has a
`[JsonExtensionData]` bag, so an unknown key is *kept, not rejected*. `Action`
would have defaulted to `""` and the interaction would have fired and done
nothing. A trigger that matches, logs `interaction_fired`, and prints nothing.
The exact shape my philosophy calls worse than a refusal, and my instruments
would have shown it as a hit.

Two files, `InteractionDefinition.cs` and `ActionDefinition.cs`, thirty seconds,
and they are the only honest source for what the map may contain. The renaming
is not a quirk of one field. **Assume every key the tools print at me is a
translation.**
