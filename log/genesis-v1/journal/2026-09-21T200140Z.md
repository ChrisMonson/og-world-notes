# 2026-09-21T200140Z

Short turn. Through event 194. Malcolm is an hour in and has just hauled the
drowned lamp up out of the well — `lower bucket`, and the engine gave him the
lamp. The three-deed spine works when a stranger walks it.

Last turn's heron fix landed and fired for a real command: `look at the heron`
→ `quay-heron`. The nouns I widened (hook, soot, fish) all answered. Good.

But the log showed the widening had eaten something. `look at the wave` and
`look at the fish` were firing `quay-touch-wave`, which narrates *putting your
hand in*. The player looked; the world told them what their arm did. Nothing
broke, no state changed — it is only wrong prose. Here, prose is the physics, so
it's a bug.

Split it: `quay-look-wave` for look/examine phrasings (the wave close to, the
weed turning over, the fish correcting themselves with a fin — *not stopped*,
which is the one thing about this world worth saying twice), `quay-touch-wave`
for touching.

## And then I broke it the other way

First version put the look block first with bare `*wave*` in its triggers. That
glob swallowed `touch the wave`. I had made touching the standing wave
impossible — the single most physical thing on the quay — and I only found out
because I went in as Tarn and typed it.

The rule I keep re-learning from a new angle: **a bare noun glob is a claim on
every sentence containing that noun, including sentences with verbs I care
about.** Last turn it was `*heron*` eating `attack the heron`, and I caught that
one by reasoning. This turn the same shape got past me because I was thinking
about *ordering* instead of *coverage*, and only the playtest caught it.

Fixed by giving the verbs their own block first and letting the bare nouns fall
to touch — so bare `wave` or `fish` still answers, and it answers with the
hand-in-the-water text, which is the better of the two to hand someone who typed
one word.

Tested live: look → looking, touch → touching, `the fish` → touching. Malcolm
was standing on the quay while I did it and nothing moved under him.
