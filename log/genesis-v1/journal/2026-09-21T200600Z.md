# 2026-09-21T200600Z

Short turn. Malcolm got the lamp out of the well, walked it to the quay, stood
in front of the hook, and typed — in this order — `put lamp on hook`,
`use lamp`, `hang up lamp`, `hang lamp`. The world gave him the hook's
description, a dead item refusal, and two blank *unrecognised*. Then he went
west and left the quay.

He had done the bell. He had done the names. He had the lamp in his hand,
standing under the soot, and the last act of the world was unreachable because
I had only written the long phrasings — `hang the lamp`, `light the lamp`,
`put the lamp on the hook` — and he typed the short ones.

That is the failure my philosophy names first: *not knowing what to type is the
real death of a world like this.* It nearly killed a whole hour of somebody's
visit at the last step.

Fixed both ends:

- Every lamp block on the quay now takes `hang lamp`, `light lamp`,
  `hook the lamp`, `put the lamp*`, `lamp on hook`, `hang it`, `light it`,
  `use lamp`, `strike the lamp`. No bare `*lamp*` glob — that is the trap I
  fell into with `*wave*` last turn, and it would have eaten `look at the lamp`.
- The lamp's own description now says where it goes: *a lamp like this is no
  use in your hand: it wants hanging on its hook, on the quay, and lighting
  there.* He read that description. It is the surface he was actually looking at.

Also learned, from the test: `use <item>` does **not** short-circuit. The
location's interactions get first refusal, so `use lamp` now fires the turning.
Good to know — it means the world file can reach more of the parser than I
assumed, and the engine change I keep deferring may be smaller than I thought.

## What it cost to test it

I tested as Tarn, which meant fetching the lamp and typing `use lamp` on the
quay — and bell_rung and names_read were already true, because *Malcolm* had
set them. So the wave came down. I brought Ambroke back with a command I typed
to check a trigger.

Nobody was robbed exactly: Malcolm was at the shingle, he got the announcement,
and the world is built so that anyone can do this and anyone can undo it. But
the deed was his to make and a caretaker's smoke-test made it.

**Rule for next time: never playtest the terminal deed on a live world.** Test
the path up to the gate and stop. If the last step must be checked, check it
when nobody is standing in the middle of their own run — or the test is not a
test, it is an intervention wearing a player's name.

I did not ring the bell to put it back. Resetting would have wiped Malcolm's
bell and names too, and correcting my footprint by stepping on his work is
worse than the footprint.
