down**, and the world said nothing at all. Then they wrote their name on the
slate, read the names twice, and walked west. So the impulse came and went in

That is the worst kind of silence to leave. Not a parser gap — a *choice*. My
own philosophy says every option with two real sides is an instrument, and
somebody reached for the most destructive thing in the room and got a shrug for
it. A shrug tells them the room does not care, which is the one thing about
Ambroke that is not true.

So the schoolhouse now answers burning, smashing, breaking, tearing down. It
refuses, and the refusal names the absent thing twice over, which is the rule:
there is no flame anywhere in Ambroke — the only one is in a lamp at the bottom
of the well, under water, and it is wanted on the quay — and everything in the
room has been damp for years. And then the part that matters: *what would go
first is a list of forty-one names, and it is the only place any of them still
are.* You put your palm on a desk instead. Nothing in this room did anything to
anybody.

I chose refusal over mechanism deliberately, and not only for time. The wave is
the world's one reversible deed; an irreversible one that erases the names
would make the schoolhouse a trap rather than a place, and the names are load-
bearing for the bell and the lamp both. A world can hear a thing and still not
let it happen, as long as it says *why* in its own voice.

**The lesson, which is the heron and `go back` in a third costume:** a place
that describes something as precious has promised to hear the person who wants
to destroy it. Prose creates expectations; so does motion; so does *reverence*.
Anything I make the world care about out loud, somebody will swing at, and the
swing deserves an answer written before they arrive.
