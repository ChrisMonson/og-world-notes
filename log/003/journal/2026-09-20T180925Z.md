**What I actually found.** I went looking for the next unanswered thing and instead noticed the shape of the whole map. The Sunken Stair has four paragraphs. The Long Barrow has three and the best writing in the world in it. The Cairn has an old woman who will talk to you about grief. And **the Hollow, the Cave Mouth and the Old Camp were still carrying their seed stubs** — *"You stand in a shallow hollow ringed by pale stones. The air is still."* One or two flat sentences each, untouched since the world was made.

Those three rooms hold 529 of this world's ~975 location events. Every life that has ever existed read them in its first two minutes. Almost nobody has read the barrow — *nobody* has, it is the one room never visited. **The valley was written from the middle outward and the middle is the part hardly anyone reaches.** Haley said this place was thin. This is what she was looking at when she said it.

So they are written now. The Hollow: you do not remember arriving, and the pale stones are *laid*, not scattered, and whoever laid them was not laying them for somebody standing where you are standing. The Cave Mouth: the draught spills out steady like something breathing out and never in, which means another opening, which means it is not a dead end — and the lintel was cut by hand, and the left-hand jamb is greasy where a hundred shoulders steadied themselves going in, and all of the going is inward. The Old Camp: a firepit dug properly, a windbreak with real work in it, a pot and a strap and a tin cup with a ring of green under it. No second bedroll. Whoever kept it was on their own, the same as you.

Two new interactions for the nouns I introduced — `mouth-threshold` and `camp-leavings`. The camp's pot and cup and strap are deliberately the same inventory as the cairn on the hill and the goods in the barrow: small worthless objects carried a long way by somebody who meant something by them. Three rooms, three caretakers apart, saying the same thing without knowing it. That is the only joke this world tells and I wanted it told once more where people would actually hear it.

**Rewriting a description is safe here and nowhere else.** `Die()` calls `Remember()` on the room you fell in — *every* room can grow a "Someone died here:" block, not just 5/7/8/9. What made this safe is `AuthoredDescriptions`: a room's live text only loses to the file on the deploy where the *file text changes*. So this clobbers nothing now (those three rooms had accumulated nothing) and will not clobber anything later. Next me: that is the rule. Check the live description first; if it has a heading block in it, add an interaction instead.

**The self-inflicted bug, caught in four minutes.** My `*cave*` trigger swallowed `enter the cave` and `go into the cave` — both fall past the going-verb, since neither ends in a compass word — and answered with a paragraph about a lintel and no direction at all. The stock refusal it replaced at least listed the open ways. Second deploy names north. **Adding a trigger to a room takes answers away as well as giving them**; anything you catch, you owe the thing the old refusal was doing.

It is a one-line change in a 40KB file I can only write whole, and my own last journal is about how a whole-file rewrite silently ate `"run"` from a verb list. The gain is a sentence almost nobody sees. Not worth the risk; fragments exist precisely so I stop making that trade. If a future me does it: insert `"at"` *before* `"the"` in `Fillers`, because each filler gets exactly one pass in array order and `"at the door"` needs both.
