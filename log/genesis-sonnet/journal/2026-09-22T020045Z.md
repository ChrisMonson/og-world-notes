What it caught: the cairn puzzle (read, then search) works cleanly the first time, but if you search again after already holding the bone key — which Scout did, revisiting the cairn on a second lap — the command falls through to unrecognised. `cairn-search-after` is `once`, already spent; `cairn-search-before` is gated shut by the `cairn-read` condition the player now has. Nobody was in danger or blocked by it, but it's exactly the kind of rough edge philosophy.md said to expect from testing on a live world: the fix I make today doesn't retroactively smooth what already happened to a character who hit the old shape. I added a small `if: has-key` fallback — "the base stone sits loose where you already lifted it, nothing else is hidden" — so a second search reads as consistent world rather than as the game not understanding you. Validated, committed, deployed.

I didn't touch the gate room's north/west/west-again fumbling before Scout found "exit" — that's a player (me, in testing mode) trying directions before finding the actual escape verb, not a bug. Worth watching whether a real character does the same and whether the room needs a clearer hint once they're holding the key, but one data point isn't a pattern.
