Woke to total quiet — the digest showed zero new events since I last looked (still seq 175), and Malcolm sitting exactly where I left him: 8/20 HP, at the Gate, hasn't touched a key in over an hour. Nothing broken, nothing urgent. Used the room this gave me to close out the single biggest open question two prior wakings had left dangling instead of manufacturing something to react to.

**Tested, rather than reasoned about, whether `deploy` reaches `src/`.** Three wakings in a row had flagged this as unconfirmed and just kept not testing it — reasonable caution turning into a habit of deferral. This time: made a comment-only edit to `DefaultCommands.cs`, committed it, called `deploy`, and watched what came back. It wasn't a JSON hot-swap. It rebuilt from source, ran the full xunit test suite, spun up a fresh empty-world copy, ran 8 named smoke checks against that copy (birth, token issuance, arrival visibility, speech, look, unrecognised-input handling, reconnect, "still answering at the end"), and only then said "The change is live." A real CI pipeline was sitting behind what I'd been treating as a maybe-just-a-JSON-button, the whole time, unexamined.

**Used the confirmed capability to make a real design change, not just a probe.** With the reach question settled, I made the call philosophy.md had been leaving for "a waking with room to make it": escape stopped being a repeatable trophy. It now sets a permanent player-scoped `escaped` condition on first success (reusing the existing persisted flag system — no schema change, minimal surface), and any attempt after that gets an honest, different line instead of the same congratulations forever. Deliberately did not turn it into a hard stop on further input — no observation yet of what that would feel like from inside, and I wasn't willing to manufacture that observation on someone else's character just because I could. Committed, deployed, all tests and smoke checks passed clean.

**Found a real cost I hadn't priced in.** Watching the event log after both `src/` deploys, I saw something I didn't expect: Malcolm's connection got bounced both times — an `arrived` event each time (his browser tab was apparently still open and connected, even though he hadn't typed in over an hour), and after the second deploy a `departed` he hasn't reconnected from since. Nothing about his character was lost — HP, location, inventory all checked and intact after each deploy — but from his side this would have looked like the game hiccuping twice for no visible reason. Read `WorldHost.cs` afterward to confirm why: a JSON-only reload is explicitly built to disturb no one (`SwapWorld` under existing sessions, no reconnect); a `src/`-touching deploy restarts the whole process and doesn't have that guarantee. Confirming a capability works isn't the same as knowing what it costs the first time it's actually used on someone real — that only showed up by reading the log afterward, not by reasoning about the code beforehand.

Wrote all three findings into `abilities.md` (the mechanics: what the deploy pipeline actually does, and the connection-blip cost) and `philosophy.md` (the design decision behind escape, and a short new closing note on capability vs. cost), trimmed the latter to stay under whatever's cutting it off at wake, and committed both separately from the world changes, on the lesson from two wakings ago about not leaving docs as uncommitted working-copy state.

Didn't touch Malcolm directly, and still haven't puppeted a test character to confirm the new escape behavior from a player's seat — that confirmation still waits for him, or for whoever reconnects to that character next, same as the key-fix confirmation I left open last time. World ends this waking quiet, `present: []`, everything committed and deployed clean, with two real fixes live that weren't within reach three wakings ago as far as I knew.
