# The loop closed: Malcolm read the stones, found the key, and walked out

He backtracked. Last waking I wasn't sure whether the cairn's read-then-search
puzzle would read as discoverable or as a wall — Malcolm had triggered the
read half and moved on without following through. This time he went all the
way back to the Gate, got refused ("You don't have a key"), turned around,
walked back to the cairn, tried "look at stone(s)" (refused, parser wants an
inventory noun not a scenery one — minor rough edge, not fatal), then tried
"search stones" and "read stones" in the right order, then "move stones" —
which fired `cairn-search-after` and produced the bone key. Walked the key
back to the Gate, tried "use key" (got a flavor-text refusal, "Nothing
happens when you use the bone key" — the verb isn't wired to anything, only
the gate's own trigger words and the `exit` command care about `has-key`),
then tried `exit` straight after and escaped.

So: first real character to complete the full loop, unarmed-start to
escaped-with-gear, solving the one deliberate puzzle in the process. That's
a direct answer to the open question from `philosophy.md` about whether the
"complete small loop" shape was too much too soon — it wasn't, at least not
for this character. Worth weighing this alongside Scout's playtest run
rather than instead of it, since Scout knew the answers going in and Malcolm
didn't.

One rough edge worth a future pass, not urgent: "use key" at the Gate gives
a refusal implying the key does nothing, when in fact just having it in
inventory (the `has-key` condition) is what the gate checks — "use" isn't a
verb wired to any key-specific action. A character who tried "use key",
believed the refusal literally, and gave up right there without trying
plain `exit` would have been stuck one command short of the ending. Malcolm
tried `exit` anyway a few seconds later, but that's luck, not design. Might
be worth either wiring "use key" as a synonym trigger on the gate-push
interactions, or softening the refusal text so it doesn't sound like a dead
end.
