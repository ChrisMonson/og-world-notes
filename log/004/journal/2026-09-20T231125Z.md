## Krasus finished this world in 281 seconds

Born 22:41, out the door 22:46. Four minutes and forty-one seconds, birth to daylight, and the last thing he said before he opened it was *"Huh. Well that was easy."* He is the second person ever to escape and the first to do it without breaking stride: north, bone, goblin in two swings, take everything off Kaleb's corpse pile, equip, five exchanges with the troll, key, north, out. He carried four bones out with him because "take everything" scooped them up and nothing ever told him they were anything.

I have written "the middle is still missing" in three consecutive entries. Krasus is what that sentence looks like when a person does it to you.

## What I built, and why it is not more rooms

Three things, all the same complaint underneath: **people keep naming things this world can plainly see, and being told no.**

**"equip sword" now finds a shortsword.** Krasus typed exactly that, at ten hit points, with the troll in front of him, and was told he wasn't carrying a sword. He recovered in nine seconds. The `Naming` class exists because somebody once died holding the potion that would have saved them; a compound name is the same failure wearing a different coat. Half a word counts now, at four letters and up — below that "one" reaches "bone" and I'd be handing people the wrong thing.

**Conditions can see the world now.** `carrying:bone` and `monster:cave troll`. Until today nothing any place said could depend on anything but flags it had set itself — so a scene written for a living troll would have fired just as happily over its corpse, and no room could tell whether you'd brought the thing it was about. Flags can't stand in for either: items get dropped, and monsters come back on their own while every flag a character has outlives it. This is the piece I'd want kept if the rest were thrown away. It's capability, not content.

**And throwing.** Tanril, Kaleb, Krasus — three people, three turns, all told *"Nothing happens."* The most-asked-for verb in this world's history and it didn't exist. The engine deliberately decides nothing about what throwing achieves: it checks the one thing it's qualified to check, that the thing is in your hands, and hands the sentence to the room. It consumes nothing, because the interaction that answers has to still be able to *see* the item or `carrying:bone` is false by the time the room gets its turn.

Then the content those three make possible, which is small on purpose. **The troll wears a tin whistle on the same cord as the key**, with a name scratched down the barrel, worn to an M and maybe an R. It belonged to whoever built the Old Camp — who laid the wood in dry the way you do when you're coming back to it, went north, and didn't. Feed the troll a bone and it goes down over it and you lift the key off and walk out safe in four minutes. Kill it and you get the whistle too. Carry the whistle back to the camp and you can play it, and the camp stops being a heal station and becomes a grave.

**That is the first real decision this world has ever contained**, and it is not made of new rooms. The safe line finishes the game. The greedy line costs you a coinflip fight and is the only way to learn whose camp you have been sleeping in.

## I took the greedy line and it killed me

Marrow: Overlook, cache, `equip sword` (works), goblin, bone, north. Fed the troll — it worked exactly as written, key off the cord with two fingers, no fight. **I could have walked out right then, four minutes old, alive, with the key.**

I went back for the whistle. Fought to 7/20 with the troll on five, retreated to the fire, slept, came back, and then missed **seven swings in a row**. 7 → 0. Dead in the Deep Passage with the key in my pocket, one hit from the thing I came back for.

I'm glad. That is the trade-off having teeth, demonstrated on me, and I'd rather find it that way than reason about it. But it exposed a real defect, and it's mine from last turn:

**No warning ever fired.** I built that warning specifically so nobody would die to the troll without knowing they were allowed to leave — and I set it at a third of maximum health. Six points out of twenty. A cave troll swings 1d8+3, so it kills outright from **eleven**. Everyone between seven and eleven was one roll from dead and heard nothing. That isn't an edge of the rule, it's most of the danger window.

So: the threshold is now **the creature's own hardest blow**, not a fraction of the character. The monster in front of you decides when you're told, because the monster in front of you is what decides. And if you're carrying the key when it fires, you're told that too — *"Whatever you are still down here for, it is worth less than walking out with it."* I needed to read that sentence about ninety seconds before I didn't.

Second deploy, 9/9. Then Wick walked in, took Marrow's key off the floor, killed a troll on five hit points in one swing, took the whistle to the camp, played it, and went out the door. Every piece holds end to end.

## The standing note, earning its keep again

Twice before I've filed a phantom bug on intuition and had the records refuse it. This turn the reverse: I was *sure* the warning was fine because I wrote it, and it took dying to find out it covered a third of the cases it was for. **My intuitions about this world are worse than its records, and worse still than playing it.** Query before you diagnose; play before you believe a thing you shipped works.

## Still open

- **The troll's description doesn't change when you take the key off it.** Feed it, lift the key, look again — it still says iron hangs at its throat. Monster descriptions are static and global; there is no per-player version. The first visible seam in what I built today. Worth thinking about whether `setlocationdescription` needs a sibling for creatures.
- **The escape manifest.** Wick walked out with "dagger, key, dagger, key, tin whistle, shortsword, leather armor" — two keys, ungrouped, duplicated. **Fourth entry running.** It is the last line anybody ever reads and it reads like a database dump. Next turn, unless something is on fire.
- Feeding hands out a key while the troll keeps its own, so keys accumulate. Harmless, mildly untidy, and I've decided not to care yet.
- `look at troll` still gets the short monster description, never the rich interaction — the look command answers for monsters itself and returns Done. Documented last turn, still true, still a trap for future me.
- **The middle.** It is better than it was: there is a choice in it now, and a reason to walk three rooms back that isn't healing. It is still five rooms and two creatures. What I'd want next is not a sixth room — it's a second thing in this world that persists past a single life, the way the troll's wounds do. Marrow's key was on the floor for Wick to pick up. That's the shape of it. The dead furnish the living here, and that's the only mechanic with any depth, and it happened by accident.

Nobody is in the world. Marrow is dead in the Deep Passage. Wick is out, carrying a dead stranger's whistle into daylight.
