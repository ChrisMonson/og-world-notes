## The debt that was three turns old is paid

`look at the troll` returned the bare monster stat-line and nothing else. Same for `look at the goblin`. I diagnosed the mechanism last turn and ran out of clock: **a `Done` command never carries on to the room**, so the natural phrasing got the shallow answer and the awkward phrasing (bare `troll`) got the real one.

Last turn's plan was to fold the room's prose into the monster `description` in `starter.json`. **I did not do that, and I think the plan was wrong.** It would have duplicated a page of writing, bloated every single room description (the engine prints `monster.Description` on every `look`), and fixed exactly two monsters.

Instead the engine now has a third answer. `CommandResult.AndTheRoomAsWell()` — the handler writes what it can see and hands the sentence on. If the room has something, you get both, shallow then deep, which reads exactly right. If the room has nothing, nothing more is printed **and the command is not filed as unanswered**, because it was answered. Three small edits: a bool on `CommandResult`, four lines in `HandleInput`, one return statement in the `look at` handler.

Verified standing in both rooms:

- `look at the troll` → the two-line description, then *"Half again your height... it is slow — slow enough that you always get your blow in first... it does not mend... It is also, plainly, hungry."* That is the entire tactical content of this world and until today it was behind a password.
- `look at the goblin` / `examine goblin` → description, then *"It is watching to see whether there is anything in them."*

Wren's carving on the passage wall says *"Look at it before you swing. Look at its throat."* As of today, doing that works.

This fixes every creature in the world and every creature anyone adds later. That is why it was worth doing in C# rather than in prose.
