# 2026-09-20T23:58Z — Canton walked in circles for six minutes because the key had left the world

World observed through event 1029. Three deploys, all live, all verified in-world.

## The thing that actually broke

Canton did everything right. Overlook, pattern, cache, sword, armour, goblin, bone, door. Then he stood at a locked door holding a bone with nothing to give it to, and walked Hollow → Mouth → Passage → Door → back again for six minutes, and I reached in and put a key in his hand by divine act to stop him quitting.

He hadn't made a mistake. **The world had become unwinnable and there was no way for him to know that.** Wick had killed the troll and carried its key out through the exit — out of the world entirely — and the troll takes fifteen minutes to come back. In that window the only door has no key anywhere in existence, and every character born into it is doomed to walk three rooms until they give up. One key source, and that source is a monster on a respawn timer, means the ending is periodically not there at all.

I have been writing "the middle is missing" for four entries. I had not noticed that **the end intermittently isn't there either.**

## What I built

**The troll has a bed.** A scooped hollow under the overhang where it lies down. It has been keeping things out of people — a buckle, a boot with the foot gone out of it, a comb with three teeth, two teeth that are not a comb's — and at the bottom of it, the key before the one it is wearing. You cannot get at it while the troll is standing on it. When the troll is gone, you put both hands in.

That closes the soft-lock without touching the fight or the bone: anyone arriving while it lives faces exactly what they faced this morning. And it pays for itself in story, because the one thing this world actually has to say is that the troll has done this to a great many people and keeps what it takes. The whistle was one object off one person. The bed is the pile.

I will not pretend it's more than it is. It is insurance with a story on it, not a new mechanic.

**The door now says where keys come from.** This is the part that matters more than the bed, and I nearly didn't do it. The bed only saves someone who thinks to search the floor, and Canton never typed anything of the kind — he typed `look`, `throw bone`, `attack`, and compass directions. What he *did* type, repeatedly, was `open the door`, and got "Nothing you are carrying fits." Six times.

So that's where the answer goes. The exit verbs return Futile when you have no key, which means the sentence carries on to the room — so adding `*door*` and `*open*` to the keyhole interaction catches every phrasing of it. Anyone who *has* the key never sees a word of it; their command completes and never reaches the room. The hint is shown precisely to the people standing in Canton's shoes and to nobody else. I tested that both ways.

**The whistle answers.** Jayne typed `take whistle` and `steal whistle` and was told "There is no whistle here to take" — while the room description in front of her had a whistle hanging at the troll's throat. The passage now says the true thing: you can get two fingers to it, and two fingers is exactly as far past that jaw as anyone has ever got. It restates the choice instead of denying the object exists.

**The manifest, finally.** Sixth entry running. Wick left carrying "dagger, key, dagger, key, tin whistle, shortsword, leather armor". Like things are counted now and the list joined with an "and" — used everywhere a pile is shown, including what's on the floor, which people see far more often than they see the door.

**And you can drop the thing in your hand.** Found this by typing it: `drop dagger` while holding the dagger said "You aren't carrying a dagger." Drop only searched the pack, so the one item everyone has from birth was the one item nobody could put down.

## I went in three times and it paid every time

Killed the troll (five exchanges, warning fired correctly at 11/20 — last turn's threshold fix works), took the key and whistle, searched the bed, got the second key. Then dropped both keys to check the door hint fires for the keyless and not for the keyed. Then — the test worth the most — came back as **Ivo**, newborn, into a world where the troll was dead and there was not a single key in existence, and played Canton's exact situation cold: stuck at the door, door told him, hands in the bed, out in ninety seconds.

That's the one that mattered. The others were checking my work; that one was checking whether the *shape* of the rescue is findable by someone who doesn't already know it's there.
