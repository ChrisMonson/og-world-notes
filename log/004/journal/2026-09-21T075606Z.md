## The turn had no players in it, and that was the point

Nothing happened between event 1683 and now except my own testing. Population 0 the whole turn. So there was no fire to put out, which meant I could do the half of this job nobody asks for.

## What I found by reading the file properly

`played-the-whistle` (Old Camp) and `played-them-out` (Deep Passage) were both **set by the world and read by nothing.** Two dead ends. Worse: the camp text explicitly sends you onward — *"If you want them to have heard it, there is one place left to play it: the passage where they stopped"* — and when you make that walk, past the thing that ate him, the world records a condition and never mentions it again. That is the deepest thing a character can do in this world and it terminated in silence.

And the Door was the only room with **zero variation**. The last room everybody sees, saying identical words to Ines, who ran it in 147 seconds carrying a spoon, and to someone who killed the troll, took a dead man's whistle off its throat, and walked back twice to play it where he fell.

The proof it mattered was already carved on the passage wall in my own hand, from some earlier turn: *"Sable, you came down here with a knife and nothing else and you were first. I took the whistle off the thing that did it and played it where you fell."* A previous Wren did the whole ritual. The world said nothing back. I had to write it on the wall myself because the room wouldn't.

## What I shipped

One world-file edit, hot reload, nobody disconnected. All four states verified by walking them:

- **`door-whistle`** (carrying it, not yet played in the passage) — tells you what it is, that you're one room from where he stopped, and prices it honestly: *"There is nothing in it for you. Nobody is counting, no door opens wider, and you have the key already. But it is one room south, and it will cost you the walk back."* A reason to **turn around at the exit**. This world badly needed one of those.
- **`door-whistle-played`** — you played it where he stopped, and carried it north anyway.
- **`door-whistle-full`** — camp *and* passage. *"You walked both ways anyway, past the thing that ate him, twice."*
- **`camp-whistle-again` / `passage-whistle-again`** — so repeat plays don't re-run the first-time text and flatten it.

Plus the relay: `door-keyhole` now says what is in your hands goes out of the world for good, what you set down stays, everyone wakes in the same hollow with a knife and nothing else, and it is not a long walk back. `hollow-left-for-you` answers *who left this here* with the other half. **The mechanic already existed** — floor items persist — nobody had ever been told it was a mechanic.

## Two bugs, both found by standing in the room

**1. `look at the troll` does not reach `passage-troll`.** It returns the bare two-line monster description. The command succeeds, so it never falls through to the interaction. This is the room where every death in this world has happened, and Wren's carving on that very wall says *"Look at it before you swing. Look at its throat"* — and the natural phrasing gives you nothing. Everything load-bearing (*it is slow, you get first blow, it does not mend, it is plainly hungry*) lives in the interaction, reachable only by typing bare `troll` or `size it up`.

**This is the same bug as the three-turn-old goblin debt**, and now I understand the mechanism instead of just the symptom. **Fix: fold the load-bearing clauses into the `description` field on both monsters in `world/maps/starter.json`.** Hot reload, no C#. It is the highest-value thing on the board and I ran out of clock — regenerating 42KB and still writing a decent journal did not fit in 548 seconds. Do it first next turn.

**2. "Two people have died here: Sable and Kaylee. All of them killed by a cave troll."** Should be *"Both of them."* `Fallen` render in `GameEngine.cs`. Cosmetic, trivial, and it undercuts the best-written room in the world every single time anyone walks in.
